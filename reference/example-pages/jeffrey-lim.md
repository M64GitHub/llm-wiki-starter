# Jeffrey Lim (Pulse)

Author of [[impulse-tracker]]. In TraxWeekly he appears as "Pulse": Zinc credits "the neat extras Pulse has put in Impulse Tracker" (panning/volume swing, panning default; source: [[s-tw075-it-percussion-tips]]), and in issue #83 (2 January 1997) he replied to Pinion's tip series under his own name, correcting the NNA example, explaining `S70` past-note cut and the info page's *Tot* column, and recommending **templates** — "one of the most useful tools" — with examples for stereo doubling and open fifths; Pinion also notes that Pulse fixed a v2.09 bug combining volume-column effects with `Jxx` (source: [[s-tw081-it-tip-of-the-week]]). The TOC lists "Impulse Tracker v1.01" by Pulse in issue #42 (12 January 1996) — the first public version's announcement, not yet ingested.

## From the IT manual and the 1.01 release note

Wrote Impulse Tracker in 100 % assembler "on and off during 1995", finishing most of it in the summer holidays of 1996 (over 100,000 lines), starting from the idea of extending Scream Tracker 3; thanks Psi for ST3, Purple Motion ("When the Heaven's Fall" — *the* best S3M), Red Haze for insisting on ping-pong loops, and [[psibelius]]; keeps IT freeware for non-commercial use, asks for donations and answers questions covered by the manual with "RTFM" (source: [[s-it-manual]]). The v1.01 note in TraxWeekly #42 (January 1996) lists an order-list save bug and asks for a demo song under 32 channels and 200 k (source: [[s-tw042-impulse-tracker-v1-01]]). His own composer hints — never release your first song, echoes with `Mxx`, several instruments per sample, tune everything to one reference sample, the perfect-loop method — ship in `HINTS.TXT` (source: [[s-it-hints]]).

## In his own words (TraxWeekly #98 and #100, May 1997)

Two pieces under his own name (sources: [[s-tw098-impulse-tracker-investment]], [[s-tw100-tracking-on-impulse]]): IT exists "because *I* track"; it was released freely "so that the hours that I had spent chiselling my sculpture could be seen in full" — "easily over 1500 hours, probably closer to 2000"; the stereo WAV driver cost US$30 for non-profit use and abusive replies to that price ("30$ my asssss") led him to declare IT213 the last feature release — "I used to envy how Psi so neatly avoided any sort of responsibility for ST3. Now, I will be able to join him" (2.14 still followed in September 1997, see below). In #100 he answered Steve Gilmore: no price tags or crippled features, any contribution appreciated ("If you bother about me, then I'll bother about you"), and told the scene to stop demanding Interwave support from [[triton]]: "If you're not willing to offer something to them, then you should learn not to demand anything." Airon's MIDI guide in the same issue ends with a plea to pay for the stereo WAV driver — "a pro product at a non-pro price" (source: [[s-tw098-impulse-tracker-midi]]).

## Release dates

1.00 on 1995-12-25 ("Final Release for version 1"), 1.01–1.06 through April 1996, 2.00 around June 1996, 2.03–2.06 in July–October 1996 (mouse support, interpolation, a new channel allocation, volume and pan swing), 2.11–2.13 in 1997, 2.14 on 1997-09-02 as "the FINAL public release", patches 3–5 until 1999-04-08 (source: [[s-tracker-history-notes]]). His official site lived at users.on.net/~jtlim and lim.com.au (source: [[s-schism-tracker-wiki]]).

## Users' verdicts in the interviews

- [[ryan-sprott]] (July 1996): "i wish pulse would stop changing the damn tracker so much, and put in mouse/ems support" (source: [[s-tw063-interview-ryan-sprott]]).
- [[mental-floss]] (February 1998): "Jeff Lim has done a great job with the tracker so far, and when he just added the ability to filter it made it even better"; his wishes were a basic sample editor like [[fasttracker-2]]'s and MIDI sync/start (source: [[s-tw119-interview-mental-floss]]; see [[midi-in-trackers]], [[sync]]).
